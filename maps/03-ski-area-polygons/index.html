An outline is not a piste.

Teal polygons are OpenSkiData’s mapped ski-area outlines. Violet polygons are official Tirol planning boundaries with a 2011 reference date. The light background shows Austria and neighboring countries. Selecting a record highlights its full source geometry, including any cross-border parts. Toggle the sources to compare them. Circle callouts highlight groups around St. Anton (Rendl and Albonagrat), Ötztal / Sölden (Sölden, Gurgl and Vent), and Kitzbühel (Hahnenkamm, Horn, Bichlalm and Jochberg–Pass Thurn). Matching buttons zoom to each group; the inspector identifies its reference polygon.

A ski-area outline can include forest, rock, buildings and land between pistes. It is not a measurement of skiable terrain, cleared land, ownership, or a legal resort boundary. Some records overlap or represent parts of larger connected areas.

Missing geometry is not missing skiing. Of 375 source records tagged as operating downhill areas with an Austrian country association, 188 have Polygon or MultiPolygon geometry. The other 187 are points and are not converted into invented polygons. Some of those ski areas can be represented by the independent Tirol layer. Neither source’s record count is a count of unique resorts; overlaps are retained, not deduplicated.

Sölden is included through Tirol. The official polygon is named Ötztaler Gletscher. It contains OpenSkiData’s Sölden reference point; 219 of 223 associated piste records intersect it. This supports the association—not a claim that the older planning envelope equals today’s operational footprint. Both names remain searchable.

The source is imperfect. Country and region classification may be coarse or inconsistent, including for areas near or across borders. All selected source polygons are shown in full; the generalized Austrian border is context, not a tool for validating, clipping or reclassifying them. “Operating” does not mean lifts or pistes are open today.

Sources, licensing and method

Data from OpenSkiData / OpenSkiMap.org, © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL), Skimap.org, Who’s On First, © Mapterhorn.

The reduced ski-area dataset is distributed under ODbL 1.0. Geometry is retained as supplied: no convex hulls, artificial buffers or substitute polygons. Official resort boundaries have not been independently verified.

Official planning boundaries: Land Tirol – tiris, Schigebietsgrenzen, CC BY 4.0. All 102 supplied polygons are valid and retained without clipping or geometric union. The source layer’s STAND field is 30 October 2011; this is not a current piste survey.

Background countries and city labels: Natural Earth, public domain. Optional street-map tiles: © OpenStreetMap contributors. The default view requires no external tiles or data API.

Design follows Datawrapper’s guidance on restrained backgrounds, clear legends, useful labels and narrow-screen testing. Source colors are categorical; fill does not encode resort size, quality or activity.
